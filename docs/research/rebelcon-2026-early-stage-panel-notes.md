## Steve Grasso

- **On Jon's Heat Seeker edge:** floor information used to be "literally a game of telephone." When trading
  moved off the floor, Jon built technology (Heat Seeker) to recreate the edge he had in person. Theme: an
  edge comes from information access; when the channel changes, you rebuild the edge in the new medium.
- **How he helps companies raise:** dinners of 10–20 high-profile investors ("all buyers") to hear a CEO pitch.
- **What he looks for in a private company:** a moat competitors can't copy (e.g. Chester Kennedy's
  LocatorX: ~30 patents plus Department of War relationships), a core competency so hard others count
  themselves out, and exponential growth or a catalyst that wins contracts no one else can get.
- **Reality check:** "All of these companies need to raise cash."

## Jon Najarian

- **Where trading really happens:** Citadel, Virtu (VIRT) and DRW trade billions of shares a day through
  exchange data centers in New Jersey (NYSE in Mahwah, Nasdaq in Carteret), not on the floor.
- **Why a transaction tax wouldn't work, in their view:** matching could move to Texas or Florida "in the
  blink of an eye."
- **How high-frequency firms profit:** they see demand, buy the offer milliseconds ahead of you, then sell
  it back a penny higher, across huge volume all day.
- **SPV fees ("2 and 20"):** many SPVs lock money up ~1 year and charge 2% upfront plus 20% of profits.
- **Late-stage pre-IPO is "the exit, not the opportunity":** big names (SpaceX, Anthropic) are often fully
  priced by IPO. The opportunity is trading around the IPO, not buying late in an SPV.
- **SpaceX IPO, as he described it:** launched June 11, priced ~$135, opened ~$140–150, later dipped.
  Elon owns ~56% and is locked up until June 12 next year; the Aug 8 unlock freed ~7%. Scheduled unlocks
  are widely known, so shorting ahead of them often doesn't work.
- **Sympathy-play strategy:** in the 3 months before SpaceX's IPO they traded related space stocks (Rocket
  Lab, Planet Labs), then got out ~2 weeks before, expecting holders to sell those to buy SpaceX. Rocket Lab
  ran from ~$60–70 to ~$150, then dropped.
- **Defense-tech deal they're excited about** (name not captured): unmanned subs, boats, drones and pilotless
  mini fighter jets; 1 to 18-month expected hold; pitched as 10x–100x (a sales claim, not a forecast).

## Heat Seeker (unusual options activity)

The Najarians' algorithm scans order flow across the options exchanges for big or out-of-pattern bets that
may signal "smart money" ahead of news; analysts vet each flag before it becomes a trade idea. Market
Rebellion calls it an indicator, not a guarantee: a flagged trade can be a hedge.
([UOA philosophy](https://marketrebellion.com/news/our-unusual-options-activity-philosophy-explained))
